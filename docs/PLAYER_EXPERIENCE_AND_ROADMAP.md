# Cuefield: player experience and longevity roadmap

Assessment of the code in this change, 29 October 2026. This is a plan, not a claim that the proposed features already exist or that a hosted deployment has been updated.

**Evidence key:** **Current** means present in the code; **Verified locally** means exercised by the automated or browser checks described below; **Hypothesis** means a player need or solution still requires playtesting and measurement; **Not verified** identifies a deployment or device coverage gap.

## 1. The game today, through a player's eyes

Cuefield is already a playable top-down pool game rather than a visual mock-up. A player can choose 8-ball, 9-ball, 10-ball or rotation, select a venue with its own fees, rules and assist cap, and play a bot, quick-match another player, host/join a private room, or practice. Hot seat, correspondence play, the daily rack, a live-table view, spectator links and completed-match replays add variety. Whether quick match finds an opponent depends on other players actually being online; there is no invented population.

| Moment | Current experience | Player-facing risk / next question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Getting started | A standalone guest selects a name; the lobby exposes modes, venues, quick match, bot and practice. Hosted Val Town can use its own login. | The standalone guest's key lives in this browser. Clearing site data loses access, and standalone progress is not a hosted Val Town account. There is no standalone account recovery or cross-device login. The breadth of choices may be confusing before the first shot. |
| Taking a shot | Cloth dragging rotates aim; wide power and fine-aim bands, spin, elevation, chalk and a keyboard path are available. Left-handed settings and several prediction styles exist. | **Hypothesis:** fine control, discoverability and accidental touches, especially on small phones, are the biggest early-friction risks. Remapping and gamepad support are not currently implemented. Physical-device accessibility and touch behavior need testing. |
| Trusting the table | Shared deterministic physics drives play and the seeded shot guide. Low pocket-lip captures are distinguished from genuine off-table balls, and fouls and respots follow game rules. | A surprisingly missed shot or disagreement between guide and result can damage trust. Keep the guide honest about stroke spread, chalk, venue assist limits and rules; do not promise a perfect shot when uncertainty applies. |
| Celebrating a shot | Jump/off-table, multi-pot and winning events can trigger a zoomed slow-motion replay, with protected input for two seconds before and after. Players can turn special-event replays off. | The replay is attractive but lengthens the turn. Preserve the requested two-second buffers and input protection; test communication, reduced-motion alternatives and synchronization rather than silently shortening them. |
| Playing again | Three bot levels, six-pocket tour, bank school, three-ball sprint, other drills and trick racks offer different activities. Practice mini-games save local bests and do **not** pay coins. | **Hypothesis:** variety exists, but learning goals, long-term mastery and reasons to return with friends are not yet organized into a coherent journey. |
| Earning and competing | Server-verified online and bot results award completion, win, multi-pot and bounty bonuses; the lobby shows progression, daily goals and Elo. At least one shot is required for match bonuses. | Players need clear fee/pot/award explanations and confidence that a disconnect will not lose money. Bot logs are replay-verified, but bot commands are client-chosen: verification does not establish the opponent's skill or prevent all farming. |
| Playing with others | WebRTC carries fast peer messages when available; a server mailbox relays reliable messages otherwise. The server validates and settles shots. Players can rematch, challenge, spectate and use chat/emotes. | A direct connection is not guaranteed. Mailbox polling can feel slower, network recovery still needs broader real-device testing, and mute is not a substitute for reporting/moderation. |
| Appearance and comfort | WebGL2 renders the table and balls; there are visual, motion, sound, label and prediction settings, plus some narration and reduced-motion support. | **Not verified:** a representative low-end device/browser matrix, full screen-reader/keyboard flow, context-loss recovery and a non-WebGL2 rendering path. We do not yet know real frame rates or retention. |

The standalone app uses the real Hono API and a file-backed SQLite database, not a mock API. **Verified locally:** the automated suite covers physics, rules, replay protection, prediction, migrations, real HTTP matches and bot settlement, duplicate claims, transaction rollback and restart persistence. Browser checks exercised the built app, replay protection, two-player synchronization and a spectator. **Not verified:** the actual hosted Val Town SQLite/OAuth deployment, all mobile browsers, public-internet NAT combinations, operating at scale, or live player retention. The standalone server is a single-process/trusted-network starting point; it needs TLS, abuse controls, recovery and operations work before being treated as a public service.

## 2. What must improve for longevity

The goal is not merely more content. Players return when they can start quickly, make intentional shots, trust results, find opponents and keep progress. The items below are ordered by the player trust they protect, not by visual flash.

### P0 — protect progress and make failures understandable

1. **Recoverable identity.** Design and implement a real standalone sign-in/recovery or account-linking flow, with a reviewed provider and explicit consent. Keep existing guest tokens working and offer a safe, reversible guest-to-account upgrade. Clearly explain that hosted Val Town and standalone data are separate unless a real migration/linking service is built. Never silently clear an account after a temporary server error.
2. **Reliable settlement and recovery.** Keep server-authoritative shots, idempotent award keys and nonnegative balances. Exercise crash/restart at each point of admission, escrow, shot acceptance, payout and reconnect. Show the player whether a match is reconnecting, settled or awaiting verification; do not present a pending payout as paid. Design a fair disconnect policy that cannot be exploited for free forfeits or refunds. The standalone transaction guarantees do not automatically apply to the Val Town transport.
3. **Production safety and operations.** Add HTTPS deployment, backup/restore drills, monitoring, resource/body limits, abuse and rate controls, secure secret/session handling and a tested migration/rollback procedure. Specify the supported topology; today's standalone SQLite service is intended to run one server process per database. Do not expose the development server as a public service.

### P1 — make a first shot easy and intentional

- **Input:** add a short, skippable, in-context first-shot tutorial covering aim, power, cue placement, spin and fouls. Offer clearly explained touch/mouse/keyboard layouts, remappable keys, sensitivity and left/right-handed options; evaluate controller support as an additional input adapter, not a second physics engine. Preserve existing default controls for returning players and add a visible cancel state before firing.
- **Accessibility:** audit keyboard focus and modal trapping, screen-reader labels/status announcements, color-independent ball identification, high-contrast controls, text size and reduced-motion behavior. Test with players and assistive technology; do not claim accessibility compliance without an audit. Preserve all replay input locks, including pointer cancel, blur, orientation changes and reconnects.
- **Acceptance:** on the supported device matrix a player can enter, aim, cancel and complete a rack with each supported input without an accidental shot, inaccessible control or loss of turn state. Replay tests must still prove the two-second pre/post protection.

### P1 — responsive, legible visuals and feedback

- **Graphics:** establish real-device frame-time, memory, battery and loading baselines before adding expensive effects. Add quality tiers/adaptive resolution with stable shot readability, safe handling of WebGL context loss and an explicit unsupported-device path; investigate a simpler fallback only after agreeing its support/cost. Keep physics and score independent of frame rate. Improve pocket/ball contrast and event cues rather than relying only on color or particles.
- **Audio and feedback:** make contact, foul, turn change, connection state and payout understandable through both optional sound and text. Respect mute, reduced-motion and system preferences; avoid mandatory flashes or audio. Profile replay and prediction worker cost under simultaneous multiplayer activity.
- **Acceptance:** agreed representative low/mid/high devices meet measured frame-time and memory budgets, including replay and background/foreground transitions. Budgets are targets to set after measurement, not current performance claims.

### P1 — networking that survives real conditions

- Keep the server as the final authority; a peer animation is provisional until its sequence/version and hash agree with the server. Version messages and maintain compatibility for active matches and older replays.
- Add bounded retries/backoff, duplicate and out-of-order handling, a visible reconnect/resync state and a clear timeout policy. Test direct WebRTC, mailbox relay, NAT/firewall failure, packet loss, high latency, tab suspension and server restart; if TURN is introduced, provision and rotate real short-lived credentials rather than inventing a TURN endpoint. Explain relay latency to the player.
- Define a supported public deployment: secure transport, mailbox retention/backpressure, observability, resource limits and, only when measurements warrant it, a multi-instance durable data/relay design. Do not assume the current one-process SQLite adapter is horizontally scalable.
- **Acceptance:** under a reproducible matrix including disconnection, duplicate delivery, delayed messages and resumed tabs, both players and spectators converge on the authoritative state; no command, fee or reward is applied twice. Document the tested latency/loss profiles and remaining failure modes.

### P2 — reasons to return without undermining fairness

- Turn existing drills into a progressive skill path: short lessons, measurable personal goals, optional replays and constructive foul feedback. Keep practice rewards and leaderboards honest: local bests are device-local, while any competitive score or currency must be verified server-side.
- Introduce seasons, rotating rule sets, clubs/friends or tournaments only after durable accounts, matchmaking and moderation are dependable. Start with one small, sustainable cadence rather than a large content treadmill. Provide reporting, blocking and privacy controls before expanding social features.
- Measure the economy before changing it: award and spend distribution, inflation, queue choice and new-player affordability. Any new reward path needs an exploit review, one-time/idempotent settlement, caps where appropriate, and a rollback/migration plan. Review the client-chosen bot-command trust boundary before increasing bot payouts. Do not introduce real-money stakes as part of this plan.

### P2 — measure whether it is working

With a privacy review, collect minimal, consent-appropriate, aggregated events for onboarding completion, time to first shot, match/queue completion, disconnect recovery, desyncs, input cancellations, frame times and reward failures. Establish a baseline and compare cohorts by input/device and new/returning players. Measure day-1/day-7 return only with a defined population and enough data; do not invent current retention numbers or set production service-level targets before a baseline exists. Pair telemetry with moderated playtests and support reports. Never log bearer tokens, full chat, precise personal identifiers or raw network credentials.

## 3. Parallel-agent plan of attack

These are proposed future workstreams; they have **not** been launched by this document. One integrator owns the shared contracts and releases; agents work on scoped patches in parallel and hand them back for sequential integration under the repository's branch policy. No lane may silently change shared physics, protocol or economy semantics.

| Agent / ownership | Deliverable | Dependencies and handoff | Proof required |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Integrator / contracts** | Define versioned shot, replay, identity, network and reward contracts; own `shared/game/types.ts`, physics-version policy and cross-lane integration in `frontend/game/session.ts` / `table-screen.ts`. | Publish invariants and fixture formats before parallel implementation. Review any cross-lane or schema change. | Legacy replay golden hashes, same seeded client/server result, no double-shot/reward, documented rollback. |
| **A — input and accessibility** | Own `frontend/game/input.ts`, `keys.ts`, `frontend/ui/controls.ts` and input-specific settings. Build tutorial, remapping, focus and pointer-cancel behavior. | Consume the integrator's action-state contract; agree screen-coordinate API with B. Do not alter physics or shared session state independently. | Keyboard/touch/mouse and assistive-technology matrix; cancellation and replay-lock regressions; real-device playtest notes. |
| **B — graphics, audio and performance** | Own `frontend/render/`, `frontend/view.ts`, rendering quality settings and presentation-specific CSS/audio. Add device baselines, readable effects and context recovery. | Agree coordinates and event data with A/integrator; consume events only, never mutate authoritative balls. | Screenshot/visual comparisons, context-loss/recovery tests, frame/memory reports on named devices and reduced-motion checks. |
| **C — network and realtime** | Own `frontend/net/`, `frontend/game/online*.ts`, `backend/signal.ts`, `api-match.ts` and `turns.ts`. | **D — identity, persistence and service** | Own `backend/auth.ts`, `identity.ts`, `db.ts`, `server/`, account/onboarding surfaces and deployment/security configuration. | **E — game progression and social design** | Own `shared/rewards.ts`, `shared/game/challenges.ts`, `frontend/game/freeplay.ts`, progression/play UI and `backend/rewards.ts` / `solo.ts` / `daily.ts`. | **F — quality, observability and release** | Own shared CI/test harness, deployment runbooks, opt-in telemetry design and release gates; other agents own their lane-specific test cases. | Consume each lane's fixtures and failure models. Keep secrets/PII out of artifacts and logs. | Clean install/build/typecheck, cross-browser/device results, synthetic network/load tests, backup/restore and a release/rollback checklist. |

### Sequencing and merge discipline

1. **Foundation first:** integrator freezes contracts and compatibility fixtures; F establishes reproducible CI and baseline metrics; D designs account migration/security. A, B and C can investigate and prototype against the frozen interfaces immediately.
2. **Parallel build:** A delivers the first-shot/input path; B delivers graphics and accessibility budgets; C delivers reconnect/relay reliability; D delivers persistent identity and operations. E limits itself to offline skills/economy analysis until the identity and reward contracts are approved.
3. **Integration gates:** integrate small patches one at a time. Every patch includes its tests and feature flag where appropriate. Run the full automated suite plus two-client/spectator tests, device/accessibility checks and a migration/restart drill. Resolve interface conflicts with the integrator, not by merging competing edits blindly.
4. **Retention release:** E ships one verified skill/progression loop after P0/P1 gates pass. Measure, playtest and adjust before adding seasons or tournaments. Release gradually with a kill switch; document undo cost and preserve saved accounts, balances, logs and active matches.

## 4. Non-negotiable invariants and rollback hazards

- The server, not a peer or a client-submitted reward, is authoritative. For standalone, fee/settlement changes and their corresponding records remain transactional and retry-safe; preserve the ledger's unique keys and nonnegative-balance guards.
- Version-1 logs remain playable by the frozen version-1 physics; new racks use version 2. Prediction and actual shots use the same version/seed. A pre-update server cannot reproduce version-2 matches or solo logs: finish/cancel those sessions and account for the UTC daily rack before rolling back the engine.
- Special-event replays never alter authoritative state and retain two seconds of protected input before and after; online/spectator views must catch up after replay.
- Do not discard an existing guest key or migrate balances until recovery and identity-linking paths have been tested. Back up the database before schema changes; verify restore, not just backup creation. Hosted Val Town data and OAuth are separate and require a real hosted test.
- The fixed-step simulation is bounded (at most 40 simulated seconds per shot and 96 contact iterations per step); per collision pass the broad phase is O(N² + N·G). New effects, inputs or transport code must not turn untrusted packets, replays or simulations into unbounded queues or loops.
